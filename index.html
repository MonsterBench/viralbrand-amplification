Match the plan to the goal

Same content engine, different definition of success.

Every campaign starts with a clear goal. Choose one to see the objective, what to measure, where people should land, and what the numbers can and cannot tell you.

Ecommerce

Objective

Acquire new customers at an economic cost, using approved creator content, not simply buy views.

What we track

  • New-customer orders and share of orders
  • Cost to acquire a new customer
  • Contribution economics after media and creator costs
  • Platform reporting reconciled with actual store sales

Conversion destination

Product page, collection or offer page into checkout, reviewed for friction before spend scales.

Measurement caveats

High reported ROAS alone does not prove retargeting or retention purchases would not have happened anyway. Existing buyers are reported as retention, not new-customer acquisition. Creator commissions apply only where the economics suit.

Lead generation

Objective

Generate qualified leads that turn into real conversations and customers, not just a high volume of form fills.

What we track

  • Lead quality: qualified, contacted and booked rates
  • CRM outcomes such as opportunities and closed business
  • Cost per qualified lead, not cost per form
  • Feedback from the sales team on lead fit

Conversion destination

Lead form, booking page or enquiry path that passes source information into the client’s CRM.

Measurement caveats

Raw form submissions are not the result. Lead quality depends on CRM follow-up, which can lag the ad data, so judge the campaign on CRM outcomes once they are available. Fixed or hybrid creator fees usually fit better than sales commission.

Entertainment / subscribers

Objective

Agree the desired action first: subscriptions, registrations, ticket purchases, viewing or another outcome.

What we track

  • Measured: verified subscribers and ticket sales
  • Platform-attributed: results the ad platform reports
  • Directional: clicks, views and watch time
  • Formal lift studies, separately scoped where feasible

Conversion destination

YouTube channel, ticketing site or registration page, chosen to match the agreed action.

Measurement caveats

A click to YouTube or a ticketing site is not automatically a verified subscription or sale. Source-level Meta subscription attribution is not guaranteed, so measured, platform-attributed and directional results are always labelled separately. Fixed creator fees often suit these campaigns better than commission.

Operating approach

Five steps, in plain English.

  1. Set up creator deals that can lead to performance

    Where it suits the brand, offer an affiliate or commission arrangement tied to agreed, trackable sales.

    • Commission is not universal. Use a fixed fee, commission, or a combination, depending on the deliverables and the brand’s economics.
    • Clarify before launch whether paid-ad sales qualify for commission, and set rules so no sale is paid twice.
    • Subscriber or awareness campaigns often suit fixed fees better than sales commission.

  2. Secure the rights to use content as ads

    Permission to post organically is not permission to advertise with someone’s video or likeness.

    • Agree platforms, term, territory, editing, music and third-party rights, and any creator-account authorization.
    • When authorized, ads can run from the brand’s handle or from a creator’s handle.
    • Organic winners are good candidates, not a prerequisite. Paid tests decide what works as an ad.

  3. Use paid acquisition to reach new customers

    Build around the real objective and the conversion signals available.

    • Use a simple account structure, a meaningful variety of creative, sensible customer exclusions, and agreed spend and efficiency limits.
    • Test the source videos and, within the agreed scope, hooks, cutdowns, copy and calls to action.

  4. Reconnect with the right people, and keep retargeting separate from retention

    • Retargeting: eligible interested non-buyers who visited, engaged or took a meaningful step.
    • Retention: existing buyers, treated separately and never counted as new-customer acquisition.
    • No automatic retention budget. Each budget has to earn its place.

  5. Make the conversion path and measurement match the promise

    • Review the offer, landing page, checkout or signup path, and tracking before scaling, and recommend fixes.
    • Separate measured conversions, platform-attributed results and directional indicators.
    • Landing-page builds, substantial funnel work and lifecycle email are separate unless expressly included.

Scope guardrails

Start small and keep the pieces separate.

One campaign, one primary channel

Start with one defined campaign. Add channels only when the objective, budget and capacity justify them.

Extra channels are scoped separately

Each additional channel gets its own scope rather than being assumed.

Separate cost lines

Keep media spend, creator compensation, usage fees, agency management, and production visible as separate budget lines.

Bigger builds are separate

Major editing, funnels and landing-page builds sit outside the base scope unless expressly included.
